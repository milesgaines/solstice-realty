Why it wins

Four things the category doesn't do

Scan before, not during

The call starts informed

Every competitor burns the first ten minutes on “tell me about your skin.” BeMeh runs the guided capture beforehand and hands the esthetician a read — history, deltas, current flare — the moment she connects.

Lighting normalizer

Comparable photos, finally

On-device white balance and exposure correction against your first capture. A bathroom at night and a kitchen at noon resolve to the same baseline, so progress is measured rather than felt.

Live annotation

You keep the map

She draws on your scan while you talk; you leave with a marked diagram and her voice notes attached to each area. It's the takeaway a facial never gives you.

Licensed only

State license verified at onboarding, matched to your state. Anything past an esthetician's scope routes to a dermatology referral instead of being quietly answered.

Build notes

How it's put together

Client

SwiftUI, iOS 18 minimum. Native camera path via AVFoundation so the capture pipeline gets raw frames for correction before compression.

Scan

Vision framework for face landmarks and angle validation; Core Image for the white-balance normalization. Captures stay on device unless the client attaches them to a session.

Video

LiveKit or Twilio Video for the session, with a shared annotation layer synced over a data channel. Recording is off by default and requires both parties to opt in.

Privacy

Face imagery is health-adjacent and should be treated that way: encrypted at rest, per-session access grants for the esthetician, one-tap delete that actually purges. No training on client photos — say it in the App Store listing.

Positioning

Not telederm and not a skincare quiz. The pitch is the treatment room relationship — a specific person who knows your face — delivered at the frequency a facial can't reach.
